# Momentum

**Definition:** A measure of an object's motion, equal to the product of its mass and velocity, that is conserved in isolated systems.

## How It Works
- Momentum (p = mv) is a vector, so it has both magnitude and direction, and a net external force is required to change it
- In any closed system, total momentum before a collision or interaction equals total momentum after, even though kinetic energy may not be conserved

## Why It Matters
- Conservation of momentum lets engineers and physicists predict outcomes of collisions, rocket propulsion, and particle interactions without needing to know internal forces

## Example
When two billiard balls collide, the total momentum of the pair immediately before impact equals the total momentum immediately after, even as each ball's individual speed and direction change.
