# Simple Harmonic Motion

**Definition:** A repetitive back-and-forth motion in which the restoring force on an object is directly proportional to its displacement from equilibrium.

## How It Works
- The restoring force always points back toward equilibrium (F = -kx), producing smooth, periodic oscillation with a constant period regardless of amplitude
- Systems like a mass on a spring or a small-angle pendulum exchange kinetic and potential energy continuously as they swing through the equilibrium point

## Why It Matters
- Simple harmonic motion is the foundation for understanding clocks, musical instruments, molecular vibrations, and how engineers design shock absorbers and vibration-damping systems

## Example
A mass hanging from a spring, when pulled down and released, bobs up and down in simple harmonic motion, with its speed maximal at the equilibrium point and zero at the extremes.
