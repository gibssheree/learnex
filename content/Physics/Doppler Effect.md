# Doppler Effect

**Definition:** The change in observed frequency of a wave caused by relative motion between the source and the observer.

## How It Works
- When a wave source moves toward an observer, successive wave crests are emitted closer together, raising the perceived frequency; moving away spreads them out and lowers it
- The same principle applies to sound, light, and other waves, though the amount of shift depends on wave speed and relative velocity

## Why It Matters
- The effect enables speed radar, astronomical measurements of galaxies moving away from Earth, and medical ultrasound imaging of blood flow

## Example
An ambulance siren sounds higher-pitched as it approaches and drops in pitch as it passes and moves away, a direct demonstration of the Doppler effect.
