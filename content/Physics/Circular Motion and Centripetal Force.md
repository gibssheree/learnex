# Circular Motion and Centripetal Force

**Definition:** Circular motion is movement along a curved path at constant radius, maintained by centripetal force directed toward the center of the circle.

## How It Works
- An object moving in a circle constantly changes direction, which means it accelerates even at constant speed; this acceleration points toward the center and is called centripetal acceleration
- Centripetal force, which can come from tension, gravity, or friction, supplies this inward pull; without it, the object would move off in a straight line

## Why It Matters
- These principles explain planetary orbits, the operation of centrifuges, and why cars need friction to turn safely on curved roads

## Example
A ball twirled on a string moves in a circle because the string's tension continuously pulls it toward your hand, providing the centripetal force.
