# Fluid Dynamics and Bernoulli's Principle

**Definition:** Fluid dynamics studies how liquids and gases move; Bernoulli's principle states that faster-flowing fluid exerts lower pressure than slower-flowing fluid.

## How It Works
- As a fluid speeds up through a narrower passage or over a curved surface, its kinetic energy increases while its pressure energy decreases, keeping total energy along a streamline constant
- Viscosity, turbulence, and flow rate (via the continuity equation) together determine whether a fluid moves smoothly (laminar) or chaotically (turbulent)

## Why It Matters
- Bernoulli's principle explains how airplane wings generate lift, how carburetors and spray bottles work, and how blood flow changes through narrowed arteries

## Example
Air moving faster over an airplane wing's curved upper surface creates lower pressure there than beneath the wing, producing the net upward force known as lift.
