# Elasticity and Hooke's Law

**Definition:** Elasticity is a material's ability to return to its original shape after deformation; Hooke's Law states that the force needed to stretch or compress a spring is proportional to the displacement.

## How It Works
- Hooke's Law (F = -kx) holds only within a material's elastic limit, where the spring constant k measures stiffness; beyond that limit, deformation becomes permanent
- Stretching or compressing an elastic object stores potential energy, which is released as the object returns toward its natural shape

## Why It Matters
- Hooke's Law underlies the design of springs, suspension systems, structural materials, and precision instruments like force gauges and scales

## Example
A car's suspension spring compresses in proportion to the weight and road bumps it absorbs, storing energy that it then releases to smooth out the ride.
