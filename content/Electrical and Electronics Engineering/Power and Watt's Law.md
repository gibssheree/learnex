# Power and Watt's Law

**Definition:** Watt's Law states that electrical power equals voltage multiplied by current: P = V × I, measured in watts.

## How It Works
- Combined with Ohm's Law, power can also be expressed as P = I²R or P = V²/R, letting you calculate it from any two known quantities
- Power represents the rate energy is transferred or converted, whether into heat, light, or motion

## Why It Matters
- It's used to size wiring and fuses, estimate battery life, and calculate the heat a component must dissipate

A 60W light bulb on a 120V circuit draws 0.5A of current (60 / 120 = 0.5 A).
