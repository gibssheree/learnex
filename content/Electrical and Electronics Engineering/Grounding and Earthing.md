# Grounding and Earthing

**Definition:** Grounding (or earthing) is the practice of connecting a circuit's reference point, and often exposed metal parts, to a common zero-voltage reference, typically the earth itself.

## How It Works
- A shared ground gives every voltage measurement in a circuit a common reference point of 0V, so signals can be compared meaningfully
- Safety earthing connects a device's metal chassis to ground so that a fault current has a low-resistance path to trip a breaker instead of shocking a user

## Why It Matters
- Proper grounding prevents electric shock hazards and reduces electrical noise that can corrupt sensitive signals

## Example
A washing machine's metal casing is grounded so that if a live wire ever touches it, the breaker trips instantly instead of the casing becoming dangerous to touch.
