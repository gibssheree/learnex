# Operational Amplifier (Op-Amp)

**Definition:** An operational amplifier is a high-gain integrated circuit that amplifies the voltage difference between its two inputs.

## How It Works
- It has two inputs, inverting and non-inverting, and one output, amplifying their difference by a very large factor
- External feedback resistors are used to tame that raw gain into precise, predictable behavior, such as fixed amplification or summing signals

## Why It Matters
- Op-amps are building blocks for analog signal processing: amplifiers, filters, comparators, and sensor signal conditioning

## Example
A microphone's weak signal is boosted by an op-amp circuit with feedback resistors set to give a gain of 100 before further processing.
