# Digital vs Analog Signal

**Definition:** A digital signal represents information as discrete steps, typically binary high/low levels, while an analog signal represents information as a continuously varying quantity.

## How It Works
- Digital signals are read as one of a fixed set of states, making them more robust to noise since small variations get rounded to the nearest valid level
- Analog signals can take any value within a range, capturing continuous phenomena like sound pressure or temperature directly

## Why It Matters
- Real-world signals are analog, so converting between the two is central to how computers sense and produce the physical world

## Example
A microphone produces an analog voltage that varies with sound waves; an analog-to-digital converter samples it into a stream of binary numbers for a computer to process.
