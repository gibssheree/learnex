# Rectifiers

**Definition:** A rectifier is a circuit that converts alternating current (AC) into direct current (DC) by allowing current to flow in only one direction.

## How It Works
- Diodes block current in one direction and pass it in the other, so arranging them in a half-wave or full-wave (bridge) configuration flips or blocks the negative half of an AC waveform
- The result is a pulsing DC output; a smoothing capacitor is typically added afterward to reduce ripple into a steadier voltage

## Why It Matters
- Nearly every DC power supply, from phone chargers to computer power supplies, starts by rectifying AC mains power before regulating and filtering it

## Example
A phone charger uses a bridge rectifier made of four diodes to turn the 120V AC from a wall outlet into pulsing DC before a regulator smooths it to a clean 5V.
