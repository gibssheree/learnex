# Inductor

**Definition:** An inductor is a passive component, usually a coil of wire, that stores energy in a magnetic field when current flows through it.

## How It Works
- Current through the coil generates a magnetic field; any change in that current induces a voltage that opposes the change, a property called inductance
- This opposition makes inductors resist rapid changes in current while allowing steady current to pass freely

## Why It Matters
- Inductors are essential for filtering, energy storage in power converters, and tuning circuits alongside capacitors

## Example
A power supply uses an inductor to smooth out ripples in current, storing energy briefly during spikes and releasing it during dips.
