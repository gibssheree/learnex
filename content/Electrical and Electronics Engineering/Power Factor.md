# Power Factor

**Definition:** Power factor is the ratio of real power (actually doing useful work) to apparent power (voltage multiplied by current) flowing in an AC circuit, ranging from 0 to 1.

## How It Works
- In circuits with inductors or capacitors, voltage and current waveforms drift out of phase, so some current flows without delivering usable energy; the cosine of that phase angle gives the power factor
- A power factor of 1 means voltage and current are perfectly in phase and all supplied power is useful; a lower value means more current is needed to deliver the same real power

## Why It Matters
- Utilities and large facilities care about power factor because low values waste capacity in wiring and equipment, so industrial sites often add correction capacitors to bring it closer to 1

## Example
A factory full of induction motors might have a power factor of 0.8, meaning its wiring carries more current than the useful power delivered would suggest, prompting the addition of correction capacitors.
