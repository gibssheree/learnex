# Due Diligence

**Definition:** The investigative process investors or acquirers conduct to verify a company's financials, legal standing, and business claims before finalizing a deal.

## How It Works
- Investors review financial statements, contracts, the [[Cap Table]], intellectual property ownership, and key metrics to confirm the company is what it claims to be
- Typically happens after a [[Term Sheet]] is signed but before funds are wired or an acquisition closes, and can still uncover issues that kill a deal

## Why It Matters
- Protects investors from fraud or hidden liabilities, and a clean, well-organized diligence process signals a well-run company that closes rounds faster

## Related Terms
- [[Term Sheet]]
- [[Cap Table]]
- [[Board of Directors]]

## Example
Before closing a Series A, an investor's lawyers spend three weeks reviewing the startup's contracts, IP assignments, and financials to confirm nothing was misrepresented.
