# Seed Round vs Series A

**Definition:** The distinction between a startup's first institutional financing (seed), raised to find product-market fit, and its next major round (Series A), raised to scale a proven model.

## How It Works
- Seed rounds are typically smaller, driven by angels or seed funds, and often use [[Convertible Note]] or SAFE instruments instead of priced equity
- Series A rounds are larger, priced, led by venture capital firms, and require evidence of traction like revenue growth or strong retention

## Why It Matters
- Knowing which stage you're raising shapes the pitch, the metrics investors expect, and the amount of [[Dilution]] founders should anticipate

## Related Terms
- [[Convertible Note]]
- [[Venture Capital]]
- [[Due Diligence]]

## Example
A founder raises a $1.5M seed on a SAFE to build an MVP, then eighteen months later raises a $10M priced Series A once monthly revenue is growing consistently.
