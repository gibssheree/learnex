# Dilution

**Definition:** The reduction in an existing shareholder's ownership percentage that happens when a company issues new shares.

## How It Works
- Every new funding round or option grant increases the total share count, shrinking the slice each existing shareholder holds even though the value of that slice may still grow
- Founders can model expected dilution across future rounds using their [[Cap Table]] to see how ownership shrinks as the company raises more capital

## Why It Matters
- Understanding dilution helps founders negotiate round size and valuation so they retain meaningful ownership and motivation through to an [[Exit Strategy]]

## Related Terms

- [[ESOP (Employee Stock Option Pool)]]
- [[Co-Founder Equity Split]]

## Example
A founder owning 80% pre-seed drops to roughly 64% after a seed round that sells 20% of the company to new investors.
