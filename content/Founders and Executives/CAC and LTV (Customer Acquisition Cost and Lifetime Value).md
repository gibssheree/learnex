# CAC and LTV (Customer Acquisition Cost and Lifetime Value)

**Definition:** CAC is the average cost to acquire one paying customer; LTV is the total revenue a company expects to earn from that customer over the relationship.

## How It Works
- CAC is calculated by dividing total sales and marketing spend by the number of new customers acquired in a period
- LTV is estimated from average revenue per customer, gross margin, and expected customer lifespan, which is directly shaped by [[Churn Rate]]

## Why It Matters
- A healthy business generally needs an LTV to CAC ratio of at least 3:1, since this proves customers are worth far more than it costs to win them

## Example
A company spending $50,000 on marketing to acquire 500 customers has a $100 CAC; if each customer generates $400 in lifetime revenue, its LTV to CAC ratio is 4:1.
