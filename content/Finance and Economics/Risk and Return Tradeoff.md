# Risk and Return Tradeoff

**Definition:** The risk-return tradeoff is the principle that potential investment returns rise only alongside a willingness to accept greater uncertainty or possible loss.

## How It Works
- Safer assets like government bonds offer lower, more predictable returns, while riskier assets like stocks or startups offer higher potential returns to compensate for their volatility
- Investors choose a position along this spectrum based on their goals and time horizon; diversification can improve the balance without eliminating the tradeoff entirely

## Why It Matters
- It explains why no investment offers high returns with guaranteed safety, and it guides how individuals and institutions build portfolios matched to their risk tolerance

## Example
A retiree favors low-risk bonds for steady income, while a young investor accepts stock market swings in exchange for higher long-term growth potential.
