# Solubility and Precipitation

**Definition:** Solubility describes how much of a substance can dissolve in a solvent at a given temperature, while precipitation is the formation of a solid when dissolved ions combine into an insoluble compound.

## How It Works
- A solute dissolves until the solution becomes saturated; beyond that point, the solubility product constant (Ksp) governs whether excess ions stay dissolved or fall out of solution
- When two solutions are mixed and the ion product exceeds Ksp, the ions combine faster than they can separate, forming a solid precipitate that settles out or can be filtered

## Why It Matters
- Solubility rules and precipitation reactions are used to purify compounds, treat wastewater, identify unknown ions in qualitative analysis, and explain mineral formation in nature

## Example
Mixing silver nitrate solution with sodium chloride solution instantly produces a cloudy white precipitate of silver chloride, since its Ksp is far exceeded by the combined ion concentrations.
