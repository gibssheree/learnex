# Isotopes and Radioactivity

**Definition:** Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons, and radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of particles or energy from unstable isotopes as they decay toward a more stable form.

## How It Works
- Unstable isotopes release energy as alpha particles, beta particles, or gamma rays during radioactive decay, gradually transforming into a different, more stable element or isotope
- Each radioactive isotope decays at a fixed rate described by its half-life, the time it takes for half of a sample to decay, which is independent of external conditions

## Why It Matters
- Radioactive isotopes enable techniques like carbon dating, medical imaging and cancer treatment, and power generation in nuclear reactors

## Example
Carbon-14 dating works because the isotope decays at a known half-life of about 5,730 years, letting scientists estimate the age of once-living organic material.
