# Chemical Equilibrium

**Definition:** Chemical equilibrium is the state of a reversible reaction in which the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal, so the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant over time.

## How It Works
- Both the forward and reverse reactions keep occurring at equilibrium, but because their rates match, no net change in concentration is observed
- Le Chatelier's Principle predicts how equilibrium shifts in response to changes in concentration, pressure, or temperature, always moving to partially counteract the disturbance

## Why It Matters
- Understanding equilibrium lets chemists and engineers maximize product yield in industrial processes like ammonia synthesis, and explains how the body maintains stable blood pH and oxygen transport

## Example
In the Haber process, N2 and H2 reach equilibrium with ammonia; increasing pressure shifts the equilibrium toward the side with fewer gas molecules, producing more NH3.
