# Atoms and the Periodic Table

**Definition:** An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains its chemical identity, made of protons and neutrons in a nucleus surrounded by electrons, and the periodic table arranges all known elements by atomic number and recurring chemical properties.

## How It Works
- Elements are ordered by increasing atomic number (proton count); columns (groups) share similar valence electron configurations and therefore similar reactivity
- Rows (periods) reflect the filling of successive electron shells, which is why properties like reactivity and atomic size trend predictably across a row

## Why It Matters
- It predicts how an element will bond and react before you ever run an experiment, making it the foundation for everything else in chemistry

## Example
Sodium (Na) and potassium (K) sit in the same group and both react violently with water because they share one loosely held valence electron.
