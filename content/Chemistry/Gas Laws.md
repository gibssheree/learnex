# Gas Laws

**Definition:** Gas laws are a set of mathematical relationships describing how the pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of a gas relate to one another under given conditions.

## How It Works
- Boyle's Law relates pressure and volume inversely at constant temperature, while Charles's Law relates volume and temperature directly at constant pressure
- The ideal gas law (PV = nRT) combines these relationships into a single equation, letting chemists predict how a gas will behave when one or more variables change

## Why It Matters
- Gas laws are essential for designing engines, predicting weather patterns, calculating scuba diving safety limits, and understanding respiration in living organisms

## Example
A sealed bag of chips expands at high altitude because the lower outside air pressure allows the gas trapped inside to expand, following Boyle's Law.
