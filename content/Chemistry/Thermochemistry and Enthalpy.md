# Thermochemistry and Enthalpy

**Definition:** Thermochemistry is the study of heat energy absorbed or released during chemical reactions, and enthalpy is the measure of that heat content at constant pressure.

## How It Works
- Exothermic reactions release heat to their surroundings and have a negative change in enthalpy, while endothermic reactions absorb heat and have a positive change in enthalpy
- Total enthalpy change is calculated from the difference between the energy required to break bonds in reactants and the energy released forming bonds in products

## Why It Matters
- Enthalpy calculations let engineers size heating and cooling systems, determine fuel efficiency, and predict whether a reaction will proceed safely or require added energy

## Example
Burning methane gas releases heat because the reaction is exothermic, which is why natural gas stoves and furnaces work as heat sources.
