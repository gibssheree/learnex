# Intermolecular Forces

**Definition:** Intermolecular forces are the attractive forces between separate molecules that determine physical properties like boiling point, melting point, and solubility.

## How It Works
- Hydrogen bonding, dipole-dipole attraction, and van der Waals forces range from strongest to weakest, arising from permanent or temporary charge imbalances between molecules
- Stronger intermolecular forces require more energy to overcome, so substances with strong forces tend to have higher melting and boiling points than those with weak ones

## Why It Matters
- These forces explain everyday phenomena like why water is liquid at room temperature while similarly sized molecules are gases, and why some substances dissolve in water and others don't

## Example
Water has an unusually high boiling point for its small size because hydrogen bonding between molecules holds them together more strongly than van der Waals forces alone would.
