# Colligative Properties

**Definition:** Colligative properties are physical properties of a solution, such as boiling point, freezing point, vapor pressure, and osmotic pressure, that depend on the number of dissolved particles rather than their identity.

## How It Works
- Adding solute particles disrupts the solvent's ability to escape as vapor or form an ordered solid, which raises the boiling point and lowers the freezing point relative to the pure solvent
- The size of the effect scales with the total concentration of dissolved particles, so ionic compounds that dissociate produce a larger shift than an equal molar amount of a non-dissociating compound

## Why It Matters
- Colligative properties explain why salt melts ice on roads, why antifreeze protects car engines, and why cells shrink or swell depending on the concentration of the surrounding solution

## Example
Spreading rock salt on an icy road lowers the freezing point of the melted water, keeping it liquid at temperatures where pure water would refreeze.
