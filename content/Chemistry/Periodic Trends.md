# Periodic Trends

**Definition:** Periodic trends are predictable patterns in element properties, such as atomic radius, ionization energy, and electronegativity, that arise from an element's position on the periodic table.

## How It Works
- Atomic radius generally decreases across a period (left to right) as nuclear charge increases, and increases down a group as additional electron shells are added
- Ionization energy and electronegativity generally increase across a period and decrease down a group, following the opposite pattern from atomic radius

## Why It Matters
- Periodic trends let chemists predict how an unfamiliar element will behave and bond without needing to test it directly, guiding material selection and reaction design

## Example
Fluorine has the highest electronegativity of any element because it sits at the top right of the periodic table, where atoms strongly attract shared electrons.
