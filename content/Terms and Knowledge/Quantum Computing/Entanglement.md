# Entanglement

**Definition:** A quantum phenomenon where two or more qubits become correlated such that the state of one instantly determines information about the state of the other, no matter the physical distance between them.

## How It Works
- Entangled [[Qubit|qubits]] share a combined quantum state that can't be described independently, measuring one immediately tells you something certain about the other
- This correlation isn't caused by any signal traveling between them, it's a fundamental feature of their shared quantum state
- Used deliberately in quantum algorithms and quantum communication protocols to link qubits' behavior in ways with no classical equivalent

## Why It Matters
- Entanglement, combined with [[Superposition]], is what gives quantum computers algorithmic capabilities that have no equivalent in classical computing

## Common Pitfalls
- Interpreting entanglement as allowing faster-than-light communication, it doesn't, no usable information can actually be transmitted faster than light using entanglement alone
- Assuming entanglement is easy to create and maintain at scale, it's extremely fragile and central to why quantum computers are so hard to build reliably

## Example
Quantum key distribution protocols use entangled particles to let two parties detect if their communication has been eavesdropped on.
