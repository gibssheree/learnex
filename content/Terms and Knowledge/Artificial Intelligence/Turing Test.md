# Turing Test

**Definition:** A test proposed by Alan Turing (1950) where a machine is considered "intelligent" if a human evaluator can't reliably distinguish its text responses from a human's.

## How It Works
- A human judge chats blindly with both a machine and a human
- If the judge can't tell which is which above chance, the machine "passes"

## Why It Matters
- The founding thought experiment of AI as a field
- Modern LLMs regularly fool casual judges, which has sparked debate over whether the test still measures anything meaningful

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating "passing" as proof of understanding or consciousness — it only measures behavioral mimicry
- Ignoring that judges can be primed or unskilled, making results unreliable

## Related Terms
- [[Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)]]
- [[Large Language Model (LLM)]]

## Example
A chatbot that convinces 70% of judges it's human in 5-minute text conversations is said to "pass" a practical version of the test.
