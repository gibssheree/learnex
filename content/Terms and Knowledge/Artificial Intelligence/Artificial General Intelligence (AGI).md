# Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)

**Definition:** A hypothetical AI system with human-level (or beyond) ability to understand, learn, and perform any intellectual task — as opposed to today's "narrow AI" that excels at specific tasks.

## How It Works
- No agreed-upon technical recipe exists yet — it's a target/definition more than a built system
- Debated whether scaling current transformer-based LLMs will get there, or whether fundamentally new architectures are needed

## Why It Matters
- Frames long-term AI safety research and major AI labs' stated missions
- Distinguishing AGI from today's narrow, task-specific models keeps hype claims in check

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating impressive narrow performance (e.g., beating humans at chess or passing a bar exam) as evidence of general intelligence
- No consensus benchmark exists, so claims of "AGI achieved" should be treated skeptically

## Example
AlphaGo mastering Go but being unable to hold a conversation illustrates narrow AI — it's not AGI despite superhuman performance in its domain.
