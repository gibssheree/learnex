# Product-Market Fit

**Definition:** The point at which a product satisfies a strong market demand so clearly that growth becomes noticeably easier, users want it, retain it, and tell others about it without heavy persuasion.

## How It Works
- Before fit: growth is a constant struggle, users churn quickly, and the team is often still guessing what problem to solve
- After fit: demand starts pulling the product forward, word of mouth increases, retention improves, and paid acquisition finally becomes efficient rather than a subsidy
- Often measured informally by asking users "how would you feel if you could no longer use this product," a high share answering "very disappointed" is a common signal

## Why It Matters
- Nearly every early-stage product decision (what to build, how to grow, how to fundraise) should be organized around finding this, not scaling before it exists

## Common Pitfalls
- Trying to scale marketing and sales spend before fit is real, amplifying a leaky bucket instead of fixing it
- Mistaking a small number of enthusiastic early users for market-wide fit, when it's actually a narrow niche

## Example
A startup notices users who complete onboarding retain at 3x the rate of others, a signal that the product truly fits their need once they experience its core value.
