# MVP (Minimum Viable Product)

**Definition:** The smallest version of a product that can be released to real users to test a core assumption, deliberately leaving out anything not essential to that test.

## How It Works
- Strips a product idea down to the minimum needed to learn whether the core value proposition actually resonates with users
- The goal is learning, not launching a polished product, an MVP can (and often should) look rough
- Feedback from the MVP directly informs what to build next, rather than guessing based on internal opinions alone

## Why It Matters
- Prevents months of investment building a fully-featured product around an unvalidated assumption that turns out to be wrong

## Common Pitfalls
- Confusing "minimum viable" with "low quality," an MVP still needs to actually work and solve the core problem, just without every extra feature
- Building an MVP so minimal it can't actually test the real hypothesis, learning nothing useful from the effort

## Example
Before building a full marketplace platform, a team manually matches buyers and sellers over email to validate that demand exists before writing any matching algorithm.
