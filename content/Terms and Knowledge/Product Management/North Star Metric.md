# North Star Metric

**Definition:** The single metric a team agrees best captures the core value a product delivers to users, used to align an entire organization around one shared measure of success.

## How It Works
- Chosen to correlate strongly with long-term business success, not just an easy-to-move vanity number
- Usually sits above individual team metrics: a growth team, a retention team, and an engineering team can each affect it in different ways
- Different from revenue, revenue is an outcome, a North Star Metric usually captures the user behavior that predicts revenue

## Why It Matters
- Keeps teams from optimizing for metrics that look good locally but don't actually reflect whether users are getting real value

## Common Pitfalls
- Picking a metric that's easy to inflate without actually delivering more value (like "signups" instead of "active usage")
- Treating the North Star as fixed forever, a maturing product often needs to revisit whether its chosen metric still reflects real value

## Example
Airbnb's North Star Metric is reportedly nights booked, not signups or listings, since that's what reflects real value delivered to both guests and hosts.
