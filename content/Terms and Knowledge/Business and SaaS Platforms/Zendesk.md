# Zendesk

**Definition:** A widely used customer support platform for managing support tickets, help centers, and live chat, built around a ticket-as-central-record model with configurable SLAs and routing rules.

## Core Services & Concepts
- **REST API** — [[REST API]], covers tickets, users, organizations, and the help center, with a robust bulk-export API for analytics pipelines
- **Webhooks** — [[Webhook]], plus "Triggers" (real-time rule-based automations on ticket create/update) and "Automations" (time-based rules, like escalating a ticket untouched for 24 hours)
- **SLA policies** — configurable first-reply-time and resolution-time targets by ticket priority, with breach alerts and reporting used to hold support teams accountable
- **Omnichannel routing** — unifies email, chat, voice, and social messages into one ticket queue with skills-based or round-robin agent assignment

## Pros
- Mature, feature-complete ticketing system with deep configurability for routing, SLAs, and escalation
- Strong reporting and SLA management, including built-in Explore analytics for support-team performance
- Large app marketplace (Zendesk Marketplace) for CRM, telephony, and other integrations

## Cons
- Can feel complex and expensive for small teams; per-agent pricing plus add-ons (Explore, Guide, Talk) adds up quickly
- UI feels dated and more form-heavy compared to newer competitors like Intercom
- Heavy customization of triggers/automations over time can create hard-to-debug rule conflicts, similar to workflow sprawl in Jira or Salesforce

## Best For
- Support teams needing formal ticketing, SLAs, and detailed reporting across a large volume of tickets

## Real Examples
- Used by a huge range of companies from startups to large enterprises for support ticketing

## Use Cases
- Customer support ticket management
- Help center and knowledge base hosting
- SLA-governed omnichannel support routing
