# Mailchimp

**Definition:** One of the most widely used email marketing platforms, originally built for small businesses sending newsletters and now bundled with light CRM ("audience") and landing page features under owner Intuit.

## Core Services & Concepts
- **REST API** — [[REST API]], organized around "audiences" (contact lists), campaigns, and automations rather than raw transactional send endpoints
- **Webhooks** — [[Webhook]], notify external systems of subscribes, unsubscribes, and campaign opens/clicks
- **Audience-based pricing model** — plans are priced by total contacts in an audience (including unsubscribed/cleaned contacts on some legacy plans), which is the single most common source of billing surprises
- **Journeys/Customer Journey Builder** — a visual automation flow builder for multi-step email sequences triggered by signup, purchase, or behavior, Mailchimp's answer to more advanced marketing-automation tools

## Pros
- Very easy to use for non-technical marketers, drag-and-drop template editor with minimal learning curve
- Generous free tier for small lists (typically up to a few hundred contacts)
- Strong template and design tools plus built-in analytics on opens, clicks, and revenue attribution for e-commerce

## Cons
- Pricing scales sharply with list size, and historically counted unsubscribed contacts toward the total on some plans, a frequent complaint
- Deliverability and automation features lag behind more developer-focused competitors like SendGrid for pure transactional/programmatic sending
- Less granular behavioral segmentation than e-commerce-native tools like Klaviyo

## Best For
- Small businesses and marketers sending newsletters and basic campaign automation without needing developer involvement

## Real Examples
- Used by a huge share of small businesses for email newsletters, often as the first marketing tool a company adopts

## Use Cases
- Newsletter campaigns
- Basic email automation
- Small business marketing
