# GDPR

**Definition:** The General Data Protection Regulation, the European Union's comprehensive data privacy law governing how companies collect, store, and process personal data of EU residents.

## How It Works
- Requires a legal basis (like consent) before collecting personal data
- Grants individuals rights: to access their data, correct it, delete it ("right to be forgotten"), and export it
- Applies to any company processing EU residents' data, regardless of where the company itself is based

## Why It Matters
- One of the most influential privacy laws globally, many other regions' laws (like California's [[CCPA]]) were modeled partly on it, and non-compliance carries fines up to 4% of global annual revenue

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming GDPR only applies to European companies — it applies to any company handling EU residents' data, regardless of where the company is headquartered
- Treating "we have a privacy policy" as sufficient compliance, without actually implementing the technical capability to delete or export a user's data on request

## Example
A US-based SaaS company with European customers must let those users request full deletion of their personal data, and must be able to actually fulfill that request technically.
