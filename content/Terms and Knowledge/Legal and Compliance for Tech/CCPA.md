# CCPA

**Definition:** The California Consumer Privacy Act, a state-level data privacy law giving California residents rights over their personal data, similar in spirit to [[GDPR]] but narrower in scope.

## How It Works
- Grants California residents the right to know what personal data a business collects, request its deletion, and opt out of its sale
- Applies to companies meeting certain size or revenue thresholds doing business with California residents, not every company by default
- Later strengthened by the CPRA (California Privacy Rights Act), which added a dedicated enforcement agency

## Why It Matters
- The most influential US state privacy law, and often treated as a practical baseline for US companies since California's market size makes opting out impractical for most consumer-facing businesses

## Common Pitfalls
- Building only a GDPR-compliant flow and assuming it automatically satisfies CCPA — the specific rights and thresholds differ enough that they need separate review
- Ignoring the "opt out of sale" requirement, which applies even to data-sharing arrangements that don't look like a traditional "sale"

A California resident can request that a company disclose exactly what personal data it has collected about them over the past 12 months.
