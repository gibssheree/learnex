# SOC 2

**Definition:** An auditing standard that verifies a company's systems are designed and operated securely, based on five "trust service criteria": security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy.

## How It Works
- An independent auditor reviews a company's actual security controls and practices (access management, encryption, monitoring, incident response) against the trust service criteria
- **Type I** report: verifies controls are designed correctly at a single point in time
- **Type II** report: verifies controls actually operated effectively over a period, usually 6-12 months, a much stronger signal

## Why It Matters
- Has become the default trust signal enterprise customers require before buying from a B2B SaaS vendor, especially one handling sensitive data

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating a SOC 2 report as a one-time achievement rather than an ongoing operational commitment, Type II audits repeat annually
- Confusing SOC 2 with ISO 27001-style certification, they're similar in spirit but structured and audited differently

## Example
An enterprise customer refuses to sign a contract with a SaaS startup until the startup can produce a current SOC 2 Type II report.
