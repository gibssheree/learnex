# Information Architecture

**Definition:** The practice of organizing and structuring content and navigation so users can find what they need and understand where they are within a product.

## How It Works
- Groups content into logical categories and hierarchies (navigation menus, site maps, folder structures)
- Often validated through card sorting (users group content themselves) and tree testing (users try to find things in a proposed structure) before it's built
- Determines things like: what goes in the main nav, what's nested under a submenu, how deep a hierarchy should go

## Why It Matters
- A product can have great visual design and still fail if users can't figure out where anything is, IA is the invisible structure that makes navigation feel obvious

## Common Pitfalls
- Organizing content around how the company's internal teams are structured rather than how users actually think about the content
- Building navigation with too many levels of nesting, making things technically "findable" but practically buried

## Example
An e-commerce site's IA determines whether "Returns" lives under "My Account," "Help," or both, based on where users actually expect to look for it.
